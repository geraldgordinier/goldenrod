A screenplay reader for the way you actually read.

Goldenrod opens screenplay PDFs in the light you want, turns one click into a comment, and saves your notes into the PDF as you go. It reads the script aloud with the voices already on your computer, one per character, and breaks every script out by scene and character. One file. Runs offline in your browser. No account.

How to use it

There's nothing to install. The whole app is one HTML file.

Download the file

Keep it anywhere you like. Opening it later is just double-clicking it.

Open it in Chrome or Edge

Safari and Firefox work too, but autosaving into the PDF needs Chrome or Edge.

Drop in a screenplay PDF

Turn on Comment and click anywhere to leave a note. It's saved into the PDF on its own. Press ? for everything else.

What it does

Built by a screenwriter who reads a lot of other people's scripts after dark.

Pick the light you read in

Paper, sepia, dark or night, one key to cycle through them. One page at a time or two side by side. Nudge the text lighter or bolder until it's comfortable at 1 AM.

Page numbers are the script's, not the PDF's, so a title page doesn't throw them off.

One click, one comment

Turn on Comment mode and every click drops a note exactly where you clicked. It stays on until you turn it off, so a page of notes is a page of clicks, not a page of menus. Highlight a line to comment on that line instead.

Tag notes as structure, character, dialogue, logic, typo or love it, and filter by tag later. Overall thoughts and a one-line verdict sit at the top of the panel.

Autosaved into the PDF

Your notes are written into the file a couple of seconds after each change, as standard PDF comments. Nothing to export, nothing to remember. The writer opens the same file in Acrobat or Preview and sees exactly what you saw.

Comments other people left show up too, with a Reply button. Your replies go back into the file.

Autosave needs Chrome or Edge. In other browsers, ⌘S does the same thing by hand.

A table read with the voices on your computer

Press play and Goldenrod reads the script aloud: one voice for the narrator and a different one for each character, cast automatically from the voices already installed on your Mac or PC. Reassign anyone from a menu. It switches voices as the scene does.

Speeds from 0.8× to 4×. Parentheticals are skipped unless you want them. Apple's Premium voices and Edge's Natural voices sound best, and the app points you to them.

Broken out by scene and character

Every script gets a scene list you can jump through and a cast list with how many speeches each character has, how many pages they're on, and where they first appear. Filter either one as you type.

A reading log keeps your place, time spent, pace and verdict for every script you open.

Pay what you like or nothing at all

Goldenrod is free to download and share. If it saves you an evening, chip in whatever feels right. The suggested amount is $10, and there's no difference between the paid and unpaid versions because there's only one version.

Questions

Where do my scripts go?
Nowhere. There's no server and no account. The file runs in your browser, and your notes stay in your browser and in the PDFs you save.
What do I need?
A Mac or Windows computer and Chrome or Edge. The table read uses the voices installed on your computer; the realistic ones are a free download, and the app shows you where.
Will the writer see my comments?
Yes, if you send them the saved PDF. They open as ordinary comments in Acrobat, Preview and most other PDF apps, with your name on them.
How do updates work?
Download the new file and replace the old one. Your notes live in the PDFs and in your browser, so nothing is lost.
Can I give it to my writing group?
Please do. Send them the file or this page.
I paid nothing and feel bad about it.
Don't. The link is right there whenever you're ready, and the app works the same either way.
